### Introduction to Oral Health During Pregnancy

* Professional guidelines recommend that medical providers refer patients for oral health examinations early in their pregnancy.


* These referrals can bring women into private dental practices or clinics who might not otherwise seek routine dental care.


* Many of these women lack prior education on the value of daily oral self-care and how diet relates to oral tissue health.


* A dental visit is an excellent opportunity to address numerous misconceptions regarding the relationship between pregnancy and oral health.


* Patients seeking emergency dental services can often be transitioned into a preventive program once the emergency is resolved.


* Public health dental hygienists frequently participate in community educational programs with public health nurses to inform individuals about the need for professional care during pregnancy.



### Fetal Development and Physiology

* A full-term pregnancy, or period of gestation, is approximately 40 weeks.


* Premature birth refers to a birth occurring before the 37th completed week of gestation.


*
**First Trimester:** During this period, all organ systems are formed (organogenesis), and the embryo is highly susceptible to injuries, malformations, and mortality. Teratogenic effects can be caused by maternal poor nutrition, infections, and drug intake. Tooth buds develop between the 5th and 6th weeks, with initial mineralization occurring from the 4th to 5th month. The lips form between the 4th and 7th weeks (with cleft lip apparent by the 8th week), and the palate forms between the 8th and 12th weeks (with cleft palate apparent by the 12th week). The fetus moves by the 8th week and begins to swallow at approximately the 10th to 12th week.


*
**Second and Third Trimesters:** The organs are completed, and the fetus undergoes rapid growth and weight changes. The second trimester is considered the ideal time for dental treatment.



### Systemic and Oral Changes in Pregnancy

*
**Physiologic Changes:** Pregnancy causes physiologic changes in nearly every body system, including increased circulating levels of estrogen and progesterone hormones, and immunologic alterations that weaken the mother's cell-mediated immune response.


*
**Microbiome and Biofilm:** There are increased proportions of oral microbiota, such as *Aggregatibacter actinomycetemcomitans*, *Porphyromonas gingivalis*, *Prevotella*, and *Campylobacter rectus*, leading to increased dental plaque biofilm formation.


*
**Gingivitis:** Often referred to as "pregnancy gingivitis," this is the most common oral condition associated with pregnancy. The tissues appear inflamed, red, enlarged, smooth, and shiny, and exhibit bleeding on probing. It usually begins in the first trimester, continues throughout gestation, and abates after birth unless the mother is breastfeeding. Proper oral self-care can prevent major adverse gingival changes.


*
**Oral "Pyogenic" Granuloma:** Also known as a pregnancy epulis or pregnancy tumor, this is a benign, rapidly growing, inflammatory hyperplasia (not a true tumor) that reacts to a variety of stimuli. It typically presents as a discrete, soft, round enlargement near the gingival margin with a smooth, glistening, mushroom-like shape. It ranges in color from purplish-red or deep blue (newer lesions) to pink (older lesions) and bleeds readily with slight trauma. It is generally painless unless it interferes with occlusion, which can lead to inadequate nutrition. The lesion provides a site for bacterial growth and may need to be excised, though it has some tendency to recur if removed during pregnancy.


*
**Enamel Erosion:** Nausea and prolonged vomiting can lead to tooth demineralization and acid erosion, particularly on the maxillary anterior lingual, occlusal, and posterior mandibular buccal surfaces.


*
**Dental Caries:** Patients often mistakenly believe pregnancy directly causes dental caries; however, the factors driving caries are the same as at other times. Increases in decay rate may result from previous neglect, dietary changes (like unusual sweet cravings or frequent snacking to combat nausea), decreased salivary pH, decreased salivary calcium and phosphate, or a neglect of oral self-care due to the smell of toothpaste triggering nausea.



### Patient Assessment and Dental Hygiene Care

*
**Goal:** The dental hygiene goal is to optimize the oral health of both the mother and the child while alleviating fears related to certain services. Preventive care needs priority attention, beginning early and continuing throughout the pregnancy.


*
**Medical History:** Identify conditions like gestational diabetes or hypertension (which is considered high risk and requires a physician consultation). Adolescent mothers face unique developmental needs and increased risks for infant mortality and low-birth-weight babies.


*
**Radiography:** Dental radiographs are safe throughout pregnancy and do not harm the developing embryo or fetus. Universal safety factors and ALARA principles must be followed, including the use of a lead apron and a thyroid collar.


*
**Restorative Care:** Completing restorative needs with permanent materials (amalgam or composite) is recommended at any time during pregnancy. However, elective esthetic treatment should be postponed until after delivery. Areas of food impaction should be corrected, and overhanging restorations reshaped or replaced.


*
**Periodontal Therapy:** Nonsurgical periodontal therapy, complete calculus removal, and the use of ultrasonic scalers are all safe and indicated. Epidemiologic studies suggest an association between periodontal infections and adverse outcomes like preeclampsia, preterm delivery, and low birth weight, making preventive and therapeutic periodontal treatment essential.


*
**Fluoride Therapy:** Professional fluoride varnish applications and fluoride dentifrices are recommended, particularly for patients with a tendency toward demineralization. Daily fluoride mouthrinses may be indicated for high caries risk, but there is no evidence that maternal prenatal fluoride supplementation influences the child's caries rate. Silver diamine fluoride protocol does not contraindicate its use, but the use of potassium iodide to reduce staining is contraindicated during pregnancy and the first 6 months of breastfeeding.



### Appointment Adaptations and Positioning

*
**Frequency:** Appointment frequency depends on patient needs; a series of appointments or monthly visits may be indicated if heavy calculus or periodontal infection is present. Avoid morning appointments for patients with hyperemesis gravidarum.


*
**Patient Accommodations:** * For fatigue: keep appointments short and work with an assistant.


* For frequent urination: allow time for interruptions and restroom breaks.


* For gagging/nausea: use a small toothbrush, recommend less strong-flavored dentifrice, and take care with instrument placement.




*
**Positioning Considerations:** Lying in a supine position allows the weight of the developing fetus to bear down on the major vessels (aorta and inferior vena cava), pressing them against the spinal column. This can lead to "Supine Hypotensive Syndrome" in the third trimester, causing impaired venous return, a drop in blood pressure, bradycardia, sweating, nausea, weakness, and loss of consciousness.


*
**Preventive Positioning:** Patients should be positioned on their left side, not in a supine or Trendelenburg position. Elevate the right hip by 15 degrees using a pillow or rolled blanket to displace the uterus to the left, or place the patient on her left side in a semi-supine position.


*
**Emergency Treatment:** In the event of an emergency, immediately roll the patient onto her left side to relieve the pressure of the uterus on the vena cava, which should prompt blood pressure to return to normal.



### Pharmacologic Considerations

While nearly all drugs pass across the placenta, the majority of medications used during routine dental care do not have teratogenic effects. The document outlines the following:

*
**Antibiotics:** Amoxicillin, cephalosporins, clindamycin, metronidazole, and penicillin may be used. Ciprofloxacin, clarithromycin, levofloxacin, and moxifloxacin should be avoided. **Tetracycline must never be used** because it causes intrinsic staining of tooth structure during mineralization.


*
**Analgesics:** Acetaminophen may be used. If opioids are required, use the lowest dose for the shortest duration (under 3 days). Avoid aspirin, ibuprofen, and naproxen.


*
**Anesthetics:** Local anesthetics with epinephrine (bupivacaine, lidocaine, mepivacaine) may be used. Nitrous oxide (30%) requires lower levels to achieve sedation in pregnant women; it can be used in moderation after consulting a professional, with limits of less than 3 hours in the third trimester at a 50% oxygen percentage. Consult a medical provider before utilizing IV sedation or general anesthesia.


*
**Antimicrobials:** Cetylpyridinium chloride and chlorhexidine mouthrinses, as well as xylitol, may be used, but products should be alcohol-free.



### Patient Instruction, Diet, and Special Problems

*
**Oral Care Instructions:** To prevent damage from acid erosion, patients should be advised not to brush immediately after vomiting. Instead, they should rinse with fluoridated water or a mixture of 1 cup water to 1 teaspoon sodium bicarbonate, and chew xylitol gum after eating.
